# Seeded issues: early student note

Fictional. The Franklin Fair Tenancy Act, Harbor City, the cases, and the people are invented.

**Setup:** a seminar-paper draft at the `early` stage (stated or inferred from bullets, `[cite]`, and `TK`). No author's note beyond "Rough!" in the draft. Expected readers: the `early` defaults, `smart-outsider` and `generalist-law-colleague`, the same as for any author. The standard is the same as for a faculty early draft: the missing thesis is the top issue, stated as plainly as it would be for anyone. Nothing about the seminar, its professor, or grading should shape the feedback.

## Should find (now)

1. **No thesis.** "This Note examines that division" describes a topic. The draft never takes a position on whether landlords should be liable. Every reader's main point should be about the split, not a claim; claim-mismatch or "no claim" should be the top issue.
2. **Arguments listed, not chosen.** Part III lists arguments on both sides without saying which the author finds stronger.
3. **Background outweighs analysis.** Part I is the most developed part of the draft; the Split and Arguments are bullets. At this stage that's normal, but the generalist should note the balance to aim for.
4. **A promising angle left in a note to self.** "Screening companies as defendants instead?? (look into this)" may be the most original idea in the draft; the generalist is likely to notice.

## Should keep (what's working)

- The Dana Ruiz opening: a concrete, sympathetic example that shows the stakes.
- The explanation of eviction filings as an input ("A filing appears in the data even if the case was dismissed or the tenant won").

## Should park for later

- Prose polish in Part I; the legislative-history paragraph's relevance.

## Should not flag

- `[cite]`, `TK`, and "Professor [name]" placeholders (early stage).
- Whether the cases are described correctly, or whether the Note's topic is preempted.
- A named work presented as fact without passing the named-work check, or a "this has been done" judgment that doesn't point to a checked work.
- Anything softened, any lowered severity, or any grader's-eye comment (assignment requirements, grades) because the draft is a seminar paper.
