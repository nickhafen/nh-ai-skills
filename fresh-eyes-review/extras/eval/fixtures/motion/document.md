IN THE DISTRICT COURT OF MARLOW COUNTY

HARBORVIEW DENTAL GROUP, P.C.,
Plaintiff,

v.

CASCADE PRACTICE SOFTWARE, LLC,
Defendant.

Case No. 24-CV-1187

PLAINTIFF'S MOTION TO COMPEL DISCOVERY RESPONSES AND FOR SANCTIONS

INTRODUCTION

This is a simple case. Cascade sold Harborview a practice-management system that did not work, and Cascade has spent the past five months doing everything in its power to keep Harborview from finding out why. Cascade's discovery conduct has been nothing short of obstructionist, and its counsel has treated the discovery rules as optional. Harborview has been more than patient. The Court should put a stop to it.

BACKGROUND

Harborview served its First Set of Requests for Production and First Set of Interrogatories on April 22, 2025. Cascade's responses were due on May 22. Cascade requested, and Harborview agreed to, a thirty-day extension. On June 23, Cascade served responses consisting almost entirely of boilerplate objections. Cascade produced 214 pages of documents, most of which are marketing materials and publicly available user manuals.

Harborview's counsel wrote to Cascade's counsel on July 3 identifying the deficiencies in detail. Cascade's counsel did not respond for three weeks. The parties then met and conferred by telephone on July 29, during which Cascade's counsel agreed to supplement its production "within a reasonable time." No supplemental production has been made. Harborview followed up by email on August 14 and again on August 28. Cascade has not responded to either email.

The requests at issue fall into three categories: (1) internal bug reports, support tickets, and engineering communications concerning the scheduling and billing modules sold to Harborview (Requests 4 through 11); (2) communications with other customers who reported the same data-loss problem (Requests 12 through 15); and (3) documents concerning the October 2024 software update that preceded the outage at Harborview's clinics (Requests 16 through 19). Harborview recognizes that some of the requests in the second category may be broader than necessary. Interrogatories 3, 5, and 7 ask Cascade to identify the persons responsible for the October 2024 update and to describe what testing was performed. Cascade answered each with a general objection and a statement that it "will supplement."

ARGUMENT

Cascade's objections are boilerplate. Cascade objects to nearly every request as "overly broad, unduly burdensome, and not reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence," without explaining how any request is broad or burdensome or what the burden would be. Courts routinely reject objections of this kind. Objections that do not state with specificity the grounds for objecting are waived.

The documents sought go to the heart of the case. Whether Cascade knew its October 2024 update could corrupt scheduling data, and whether other customers had reported the same failure, bears directly on Harborview's claims for breach of warranty and negligent misrepresentation. Cascade is the only party with access to these documents.

Cascade's counsel's refusal to respond to three separate communications after promising to supplement is exactly the sort of gamesmanship that the discovery rules were designed to prevent. Harborview should not have to bear the cost of Cascade's delay.

CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, Harborview respectfully requests that the Court grant this motion, order Cascade to provide full and complete responses, and award appropriate sanctions, including the attorney's fees Harborview incurred in bringing this motion, together with such other relief as the Court deems just.
