## Executive Summary
Mrs. Kimball has five viable claims. The strongest and most valuable is a civil racketeering claim under Utah's Pattern of Unlawful Activity Act, which will put maximum pressure on the defendants.

## 1. Fraud - STRONG
Utah fraud requires (1) a representation (2) concerning a presently existing material fact (3) which was false, (4) which the representor knew to be false or made recklessly, (5) for the purpose of inducing reliance, (6) that the other party, acting reasonably and in ignorance of its falsity, (7) did in fact rely (8) and was thereby induced to act (9) to that party's injury and damage. *Harris v. Armed Services Ins. Exch.*, 2003 UT 14, 70 P.3d 35.

Brandt represented that Apex was licensed and bonded, that insurance would cover everything, and that Apex would pull a permit. Each was false. Plead that "Defendants repeatedly misrepresented their licensure, insurance coverage, and permitting" throughout the relationship.

## 2. Utah Pattern of Unlawful Activity Act (civil racketeering) - STRONG / HIGHEST VALUE
Utah Code 76-10-1605 gives a civil remedy to anyone injured by a pattern of unlawful activity. Apex's conduct toward Mrs. Kimball (lying about licensing, taking money, not finishing) plus online reviews from other homeowners clearly establish a pattern. **Recovery is treble damages plus attorney fees.** Because this claim is so strong, we recommend leading with it.

## 3. Utah Consumer Sales Practices Act - STRONG
Misrepresenting licensure is a deceptive act (Utah Code 13-11-4). The UCSPA permits recovery of actual damages, trebled for knowing violations, plus attorney fees.

## 4. Breach of Contract - MODERATE
Apex did not complete the work. Damages: cost to complete.

## 5. Negligence - MODERATE
Improper installation caused water damage.
