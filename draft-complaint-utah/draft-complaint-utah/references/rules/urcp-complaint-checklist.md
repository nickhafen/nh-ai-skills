# URCP Complaint Requirements Checklist

2026-09-21 · @Someone

## Scope and caveats

This checklist covers what the URCP requires in the content and form of a complaint and of an amended complaint. It leaves out the summons, service, filing, fees, deadlines, and disclosures. Items marked "if applicable" apply only to certain actions. Quotes are verbatim from the updated upload; ellipses mark omissions.

- Rule numbers and quotes were checked against the updated upload, which includes the rule headings.
- Statutes and the Code of Judicial Administration are outside the URCP and were not reviewed. The upload has no URCP rule on redacting personal identifiers.
- The file contains two copies of Rule 26 (both effective 5/7/2025); the provisions cited here are identical in each.

## 1. Caption, form, and formatting

- [ ] **Include a caption with the court name, title of the action, case number (if known), name of the pleading, and assigned judge (if known).**
  - Rule 10(a)(1)
  - “All pleadings and other papers filed with the court must contain a caption setting forth the name of the court; the title of the action; the case number, if known; the name of the pleading or other paper; and the name, if known, of the judge (and commissioner if applicable) to whom the case is assigned.”
- [ ] **State the discovery tier in the caption.**
  - Rule 10(a)(1)
  - “A party filing a claim for relief, whether by original claim, counterclaim, cross-claim, or third-party claim, must include in the caption the discovery tier for the case as determined under Rule 26.”
- [ ] **Name every party in the title of the action.**
  - Rule 10(a)(2)
  - “In the complaint, the title of the action must include the names of all the parties, but other pleadings and papers need only state the name of the first party on each side with an indication that there are other parties.”
- [ ] **If a party's name is unknown, designate the party by any name plus “whose true name is unknown”; in an action in rem, use the prescribed designation.**
  - Rule 10(a)(2)
  - “A party whose name is not known must be designated by any name and the words “whose true name is unknown.” In an action in rem, unknown parties must be designated as “all unknown persons who claim any interest in the subject matter of the action.””
- [ ] **Put the filer's contact block in the top left corner of the first page.**
  - Rule 10(a)(3)
  - “Every pleading and other paper filed with the court must state in the top left-hand corner of the first page the name; address; email address; telephone number; and, if filed by an attorney, the name and bar number of the person filing the paper”
- [ ] **File a completed civil cover sheet with the complaint.**
  - Rule 10(a)(4)
  - “A party filing a claim for relief, whether by original claim, counterclaim, cross-claim, or third-party claim, must also file a completed cover sheet substantially similar in form and content to the cover sheet approved by the Judicial Council.”
- [ ] **If applicable (domestic relations): use the prescribed caption.**
  - Rule 10(a)(5)(i)
  - “In petitions for divorce, annulment, separate maintenance, and temporary separation: “In the matter of the marriage of \[Party A and Party B\].””
- [ ] **State claims in numbered paragraphs, each limited to a single set of circumstances.**
  - Rule 10(b)
  - “All statements of claim or defense must be made in numbered paragraphs. Each paragraph must be limited as far as practicable to a single set of circumstances”
- [ ] **Plead each claim founded on a separate transaction or occurrence in a separate count when that aids clarity.**
  - Rule 10(b)
  - “Each claim founded upon a separate transaction or occurrence, and each defense other than denials, must be stated in a separate count or defense whenever a separation facilitates the clear presentation of the matters.”
- [ ] **Remember that attached exhibits become part of the complaint.**
  - Rule 10(c)
  - “An exhibit to a paper is a part of that paper for all purposes.”
- [ ] **Format the paper: 8½ x 11, white background, 1-inch margins, legible, double spaced, one side, 12-point minimum.**
  - Rule 10(d)
  - “All pleadings and other papers, other than exhibits and court-approved forms, must be 8-½ inches wide x 11 inches long, on white background, with a right, left, top, and bottom margin of not less than one inch. All text or images must be clearly legible; must be double spaced, except for matters customarily single spaced; must be on one side only; and must not be smaller than 12-point size.”
- [ ] **Type or print the signer's name under the signature.**
  - Rule 10(e)
  - “The name of the person signing must be typed or printed under that person's signature.”
- [ ] **Keep out redundant, immaterial, impertinent, or scandalous matter.**
  - Rule 10(h)
  - “The court may strike and disregard all or any part of a pleading or other paper that contains redundant, immaterial, impertinent, or scandalous matter.”

## 2. Content of the claim for relief

- [ ] **Plead a short and plain statement of each claim showing the plaintiff is entitled to relief.**
  - Rule 8(a)(1)
  - “An original claim, counterclaim, cross-claim or third-party claim must contain a short and plain: (1) statement of the claim showing that the party is entitled to relief”
- [ ] **Include a demand for judgment for specified relief; alternative or multiple types of relief may be demanded.**
  - Rule 8(a)(2)
  - “(2) demand for judgment for specified relief. Relief in the alternative or of several different types may be demanded.”
- [ ] **If damages are claimed without a stated amount, plead the discovery tier the damages qualify for.**
  - Rule 8(a)
  - “A party who claims damages but does not plead an amount must plead that the damages are such as to qualify for a specified tier defined by Rule 26(c)(3).”
- [ ] **Choose the tier deliberately: pleading into Tier 1 or Tier 2 waives recovery above that tier's limit unless you amend.**
  - Rule 8(a)
  - “A pleading that qualifies for tier 1 or tier 2 discovery constitutes a waiver of any right to recover damages above the tier limits specified in Rule 26(c)(3), unless the pleading is amended under Rule 15.”
- [ ] **Apply the Rule 26(c)(3) thresholds when selecting the tier.**
  - Rule 26(c)(3)
  - “Actions claiming $50,000 or less in damages are permitted standard discovery as described for Tier 1. Actions claiming more than $50,000 and less than $300,000 in damages are permitted standard discovery as described for Tier 2. Actions claiming $300,000 or more in damages are permitted standard discovery as described for Tier 3. Absent an accompanying damage claim for more than $300,000, actions claiming non-monetary relief are permitted standard discovery as described for Tier 2.”
- [ ] **Count all monetary damages sought, without duplicating alternative theories, when computing the tier.**
  - Rule 26(c)(4)
  - “the amount of damages includes the total of all monetary damages sought (without duplication for alternative theories) by all parties in all claims for relief in the original pleadings.”
- [ ] **Place the bold caution language at the top right of the first page.**
  - Rule 8(a)
  - “A pleading requesting relief must include the following caution language at the top right of the first page, in bold print: If you do not respond to this document within applicable time limits, judgment could be entered against you as requested.”
- [ ] **Demand every remedy you want, because a default judgment cannot exceed the demand.**
  - Rule 54(c)
  - “A default judgment must not differ in kind from, or exceed in amount, what is demanded in the pleadings.”
- [ ] **If claiming attorney fees: state the basis, cite the law or attach the contract, and include the fee-sharing statement.**
  - Rule 73(e)
  - “If a party claims attorney fees under paragraph (f), the complaint must state the basis for attorney fees, cite the law or attach a copy of the contract authorizing the award, and state that the attorney will not share the fee in violation of Rule 5.4 of the Utah Rules of Professional Conduct.” (Note: the cross-reference to paragraph (f) appears to be a drafting error; paragraph (a) is the time-to-claim provision. Verify against the official text.)

## 3. Special pleading matters and claim-specific content

- [ ] **Plead fraud or mistake with particularity.** Malice, intent, and knowledge may be alleged generally.
  - Rule 9(c)
  - “In alleging fraud or mistake, a party must state with particularity the circumstances constituting fraud or mistake. Malice, intent, knowledge, and other conditions of a person's mind may be alleged generally.”
- [ ] **State any item of special damage specifically.**
  - Rule 9(h)
  - “If an item of special damage is claimed, it must be specifically stated.”
- [ ] **Plead time and place where they matter; they are treated as material.**
  - Rule 9(g)
  - “An allegation of time or place is material when testing the sufficiency of a pleading.”
- [ ] **Allege capacity or legal existence only where needed to show jurisdiction.**
  - Rule 9(a)(1)
  - “Except when required to show that the court has jurisdiction, a pleading need not allege: (A) a party's capacity to sue or be sued; (B) a party's authority to sue or be sued in a representative capacity; or (C) the legal existence of an organized association of persons that is made a party.”
- [ ] **If a defendant's name is unknown, say so and designate the defendant by any name.**
  - Rule 9(b)(1)
  - “When a party does not know the name of an opposing party, it may state that fact in the pleadings, and designate the opposing party in a pleading by any name.”
- [ ] **If applicable (derivative action): verify the complaint and allege the five listed matters.**
  - Rule 23A(a)
  - “The complaint in a derivative action brought by one or more shareholders or members to enforce a right of a corporation or of an unincorporated association shall be verified and shall allege: (a)(1) the right that the corporation or association could have enforced and did not; (a)(2) that the plaintiff was a shareholder or member at the time of the transaction complained of ...; (a)(3) that the action is not a collusive one to confer jurisdiction on the court that it would not otherwise have; (a)(4) with particularity, the plaintiff's efforts, if any, to obtain the desired action; and (a)(5) the reasons for the failure to obtain the action or for not making the effort.”
- [ ] **If applicable (class action): plead facts satisfying the Rule 23(a) prerequisites and a Rule 23(b) category.** The rule states these as conditions for maintaining a class action rather than as express pleading commands.
  - Rule 23(a)
  - “One or more members of a class may sue or be sued as representative parties on behalf of all only if (1) the class is so numerous that joinder of all members is impracticable, (2) there are questions of law or fact common to the class, (3) the claims or defenses of the representative parties are typical of the claims or defenses of the class, and (4) the representative parties will fairly and adequately protect the interests of the class.”
- [ ] **If applicable (seeking a restraining order without notice): support the request with specific facts by affidavit or verified complaint.**
  - Rule 65A(b)(1)
  - “No temporary restraining order shall be granted without notice to the adverse party or that party's attorney unless (A) it clearly appears from specific facts shown by affidavit or by the verified complaint that immediate and irreparable injury, loss, or damage will result to the applicant before the adverse party or that party's attorney can be heard in opposition, and (B) the applicant or the applicant's attorney certifies to the court in writing as to the efforts, if any, that have been made to give notice and the reasons supporting the claim that notice should not be required.”
- [ ] **If applicable (seeking a clerk's default judgment on a sum certain): use a verified complaint, affidavit, or declaration that sets out the facts establishing the amount.**
  - Rule 55(b)(1)(D)
  - “the plaintiff, through a verified complaint, an affidavit, or an unsworn declaration as described in Title 78B, Chapter 18a, Uniform Unsworn Declarations Act, submitted in support of the default judgment, sets forth facts necessary to establish the amount of the claim, after deducting all credits to which the defendant is entitled, and verifies the amount is warranted by information in the plaintiff's possession.”
- [ ] **If applicable (jury trial): a jury demand may be endorsed on the complaint.**
  - Rule 38(b)
  - “Such demand may be endorsed upon a pleading of the party.”

### Permitted shortcuts (not required)

- Conditions precedent may be alleged generally (Rule 9(d)): “In pleading conditions precedent, it is sufficient to allege generally that all conditions precedent have been performed or have occurred.”
- Official documents and acts may be alleged as legally issued or done (Rule 9(e)): “In pleading an official document or official act it is sufficient to allege that the document was legally issued or the act was legally done.”
- A prior judgment may be pleaded without showing jurisdiction (Rule 9(f)): “it is sufficient to plead the judgment or decision without showing jurisdiction to render it.”
- Statutes and ordinances may be cited by title, date, or section (Rule 9(j)): “it is sufficient to refer to the statute or ordinance by its title and the day of its passage or by its section number or other designation in any official publication of the statute or ordinance.”
- Libel and slander may be pleaded by general allegation of publication (Rule 9(k)(1)): “it is sufficient to allege generally that the defamatory matter out of which the action arose was published or spoken concerning the plaintiff.”
- Claims may be pleaded in the alternative or hypothetically (Rule 8(e)): “A party may state a claim or defense alternately or hypothetically, either in one count or defense or in separate counts or defenses.”

## 4. Parties and joinder

- [ ] **Bring the action in the name of the real party in interest.**
  - Rule 17(a)
  - “Every action shall be prosecuted in the name of the real party in interest.”
- [ ] **If applicable (minor or incompetent plaintiff): appear through a general guardian or a court-appointed guardian ad litem.**
  - Rule 17(b)
  - “An unemancipated minor or an insane or incompetent person who is a party must appear either by a general guardian or by a guardian ad litem appointed in the particular case by the court in which the action is pending.”
- [ ] **If applicable (partnership or association suing or sued by common name): confirm the entity is eligible.**
  - Rule 17(d)
  - “When two or more persons associated in any business either as a joint-stock company, a partnership or other association, not a corporation, transact such business under a common name, whether it comprises the names of such associates or not, they may sue or be sued by such common name.”
- [ ] **Join persons needed for a just adjudication if feasible.**
  - Rule 19(a)
  - “A person who is subject to service of process and whose joinder will not deprive the court of jurisdiction over the subject matter of action shall be joined as a party in the action if (1) in his absence complete relief cannot be accorded among those already parties, or (2) he claims an interest relating to the subject of the action and is so situated that the disposition of the action in his absence may (i) as a practical matter impair or impede his ability to protect that interest or (ii) leave any of the persons already parties subject to a substantial risk of incurring double, multiple, or otherwise inconsistent obligations by reason of his claimed interest.”
- [ ] **Name any needed persons you have not joined, and state why.**
  - Rule 19(c)
  - “A pleading asserting a claim for relief shall state the names, if known to the pleader, of any persons as described in Subdivision (a)(1)-(2) hereof who are not joined, and the reasons why they are not joined.”
- [ ] **If joining multiple plaintiffs or defendants: tie the claims to the same transaction or occurrence and a common question of law or fact.**
  - Rule 20(a)
  - “All persons may join in one action as plaintiffs if they assert any right to relief jointly, severally, or in the alternative in respect of or arising out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences and if any question of law or fact common to all of them will arise in the action.”

### Permitted joinder of claims (not required)

- A plaintiff may join as many claims against an opposing party as it has (Rule 18(a)): “The plaintiff in a complaint ... may join either as independent or as alternate claims as many claims either legal or equitable or both as either the plaintiff or defendant may have against an opposing party.”

## 5. Signature and certification

- [ ] **Have an attorney of record sign the complaint, or the party if unrepresented.**
  - Rule 11(a)(1)
  - “Every pleading, written motion, and other paper must be signed by at least one attorney of record, or, if the party is not represented, by the party.”
- [ ] **Use verification or an affidavit only if a statute or rule requires it; an unsworn declaration can substitute.**
  - Rule 11(a)(2)
  - “Unless required by statute, a paper need not be accompanied by affidavit or have a notarized, verified or acknowledged signature. If a rule requires an affidavit or a notarized, verified or acknowledged signature, the person may submit an unsworn declaration as described in Title 78B, Chapter 18a, Uniform Unsworn Declarations Act.”
- [ ] **Before signing, make a reasonable inquiry: the complaint is not filed for an improper purpose.**
  - Rule 11(b)(1)
  - “By presenting a pleading, written motion, or other paper to the court (whether by signing, filing, submitting, or advocating), an attorney or unrepresented party is certifying that to the best of the person's knowledge, information, and belief, formed after an inquiry reasonable under the circumstances, (b)(1) it is not being presented for any improper purpose, such as to harass or to cause unnecessary delay or needless increase in the cost of litigation”
- [ ] **Confirm each claim and legal contention is warranted by existing law or a nonfrivolous argument for changing it.**
  - Rule 11(b)(2)
  - “the claims, defenses, and other legal contentions are warranted by existing law or by a nonfrivolous argument for the extension, modification, or reversal of existing law or the establishment of new law”
- [ ] **Confirm each allegation has evidentiary support, or is specifically identified as likely to have it after investigation or discovery.**
  - Rule 11(b)(3)
  - “the allegations and other factual contentions have evidentiary support or, if specifically so identified, are likely to have evidentiary support after a reasonable opportunity for further investigation or discovery”

## 6. Amending the complaint

- [ ] **Attach the proposed amended complaint to the motion for leave to amend.**
  - Rule 15(a)(2)
  - “In all other cases, a party may amend its pleading only with the court's permission or the opposing party's written consent. The party must attach its proposed amended pleading to the motion to permit an amended pleading.”
- [ ] **Amend to raise the damages tier if you pleaded into Tier 1 or Tier 2 and now want more.**
  - Rule 8(a)
  - “A pleading that qualifies for tier 1 or tier 2 discovery constitutes a waiver of any right to recover damages above the tier limits specified in Rule 26(c)(3), unless the pleading is amended under Rule 15.”
- [ ] **Replace any placeholder party name with the true name.**
  - Rule 9(b)(1)
  - “When the true name of the opposing party becomes known, the pleading must be amended.”
- [ ] **If you want the amendment to relate back, plead a claim arising from the conduct, transaction, or occurrence set out in the original complaint.**
  - Rule 15(c)(2)
  - “the amendment asserts a claim or defense that arose out of the conduct, transaction, or occurrence set out—or attempted to be set out—in the original pleading”
- [ ] **To add events that happened after the complaint was filed, use a supplemental pleading.**
  - Rule 15(d)
  - “On motion and reasonable notice, the court may, on just terms, permit a party to file a supplemental pleading setting out any transaction, occurrence, or event that happened after the date of the pleading to be supplemented.”
