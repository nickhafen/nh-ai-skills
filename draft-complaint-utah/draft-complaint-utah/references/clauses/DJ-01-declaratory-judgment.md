# DJ-01: Declaratory Judgment (Utah)
Status: Verified 2026-09-21 | Last reviewed by: Claude (AI). Attorney review required.

## When to use / when not to
- **Use** when there is a present, concrete dispute about rights, status, or legal relations under a contract, deed, will, other writing, statute, ordinance, or franchise, and a declaration will resolve the dispute or guide future conduct (for example, whether a contract remains in force, what a clause means, or who owns a disputed interest).
- The existence of another adequate remedy does not bar declaratory relief where it is appropriate. Utah R. Civ. P. 57.
- **Do not use** for hypothetical or future disputes, or only to relabel a damages claim that is already pleaded. Courts have discretion to deny relief that would not end the controversy (see Utah Code § 78B-6-404, "Discretion to deny declaratory relief").

## Statutory framework (Utah Code Title 78B, Chapter 6, Part 4; current as of 2026-09-21)
- § 78B-6-401 (rewritten effective July 1, 2024; now titled "Power to issue declaratory judgment -- Form -- Effect"): "A court with jurisdiction under Title 78A . . . has the power to issue declaratory judgments determining rights, status, and other legal relations within its respective jurisdiction." A declaration "shall have the force and effect of a final judgment or decree." Superseded versions were titled "Jurisdiction of district courts."
- § 78B-6-408: a person interested under a deed, will, written contract, or other writing, or whose rights are affected by a statute, ordinance, contract, or franchise, may have determined "any question of construction or validity arising under the instrument, statute, ordinance, contract, or franchise" and obtain a declaration of "rights, status, or other legal relations." - Other sections: § 402 (court's general powers), § 403 (parties), § 404 (discretion to deny), § 406 (supplemental relief), § 407 (trial of issues of fact), § 409 (contracts), § 411 (costs), § 412 (liberal construction). Section titles verified; text not reviewed.
- Procedure: Utah R. Civ. P. 57 (jury trial under Rules 38–39; speedy hearing possible).

## Elements (justiciability prerequisites)
Authority for E1–E4: *Jenkins v. Swan*, 675 P.2d 1145, 1148 (Utah 1983).
E1. A justiciable controversy exists.
E2. The parties' interests are adverse.
E3. {{Plaintiff}} has a legally protectible interest in the controversy.
E4. The issues are ripe for judicial determination.

## Prayer language
- A declaration that {{state each declaration precisely, e.g., "the Agreement remains in full force and effect through ___"; "Section __ does not require {{Plaintiff}} to ___"}}.
- Supplemental relief based on the declaration, as allowed by Utah Code § 78B-6-406 [[confirm text]]; costs under § 78B-6-411 [[confirm]]; other just relief.

## Alternative-pleading notes
- Pairs with BOC-01 (declaring the meaning of terms going forward) and with JV-01 (declaring the parties' ownership interests). State the declaration precisely. Vague declarations invite a dismissal under § 78B-6-404.
- If the complaint also seeks damages for the same past breach, explain why the declaration adds something, such as future performance or ongoing rights.

## Prerequisites / bars / common defenses
- **Joinder:** § 78B-6-403 addresses parties; join everyone whose interests the declaration would affect [[confirm text]].
- **Defenses:** no justiciable controversy; not ripe; lack of standing; an advisory opinion; the court's discretion under § 78B-6-404.
