# ACC-01: Accounting, Equitable (Utah)
Status: Partly verified 2026-09-21 | Last reviewed by: Claude (AI). Attorney review required.

## When to use / when not to
- **Use** when {{Defendant}} controls the books, funds, or records of a shared enterprise or of property held for {{Plaintiff}} (partnership, joint venture, trust, agency), {{Plaintiff}} cannot compute what is owed without them, and a fiduciary or confidential relationship exists.
- Treat it as an equitable remedy tied to a substantive claim (JV-01, FD-01, BOC-01), not as a standalone theory. *Score v. Wilson*, 611 P.2d 367 (Utah 1980), is an example of an action "seeking an accounting of proceeds of a joint venture."
- **Do not use** when the amount owed is a simple sum that can be proven through discovery, or when there is no relationship of trust. Courts may treat it as unnecessary.
- [UNVERIFIED] This library did not confirm a Utah case that states standalone elements for an equitable accounting claim. The elements below are drafted from the verified partnership authorities and from the nature of the remedy. Research before relying on them.

## Elements (working formulation; see note above)
E1. A relationship that gives {{Plaintiff}} a right to an accounting, such as partner or co-venturer, principal and agent, or trustee and beneficiary. Authority: Utah Code § 48-1d-406(2) (a partner may sue another partner "for legal or equitable relief, with or without an accounting"); § 48-1d-405(2) (duty "to account" to the partnership) [[as quoted by the tool; confirm the exact text]].
E2. {{Defendant}} holds or controls money, property, or records in which {{Plaintiff}} has an interest. [UNVERIFIED as a formal element]
E3. The accounts are complicated enough, or {{Plaintiff}} lacks enough access, that the amount owed cannot be determined without an accounting. Compare *Charlesworth v. Reyns*, 2005 UT App 214, ¶¶ 17–22, 113 P.3d 1031 (no accounting needed where the dispute concerned "a very limited aspect of the partnership business" and "did not involve complex accounting issues").
E4. Demand and refusal, or other facts showing the need for court supervision. [UNVERIFIED as a formal element; plead it anyway]

## Pleading paragraphs (template)
**General Allegations**
1. As alleged above, {{Plaintiff}} and {{Defendant}} are {{partners / co-venturers / principal and agent}} in {{enterprise}}. [E1]
2. {{Defendant}} has kept the books and records of {{enterprise}} and has controlled its bank account(s) at {{bank}} since {{Date}}. [E2]
3. {{Enterprise}} has had many transactions, including {{types: sales, draws, reimbursements, capital calls}}, from {{Date}} to {{Date}}. {{Plaintiff}} cannot determine the amount owed without those records. [E3]
4. On {{Date}}, {{Plaintiff}} asked in writing for {{records / a full accounting}}. {{Defendant}} [[refused / provided incomplete records / did not respond]]. [E4]

**Count {{N}}: Accounting**
5. {{Plaintiff}} incorporates the preceding paragraphs.
6. Because of the relationship between the parties, {{Defendant}} must account to {{Plaintiff}} for all money and property of {{enterprise}}. [E1, E2]
7. The amount owed to {{Plaintiff}} cannot be determined without a full accounting of {{enterprise}}'s receipts, expenses, distributions, and assets from {{Date}} to the present. [E3]
8. {{Defendant}} has refused to provide that accounting. [E4]
9. {{Plaintiff}} is entitled to a full accounting and to judgment for any amount the accounting shows is owed.

## Prayer language
- An order requiring {{Defendant}} to provide a full accounting of {{enterprise}} from {{Date}} to the present, within {{__}} days, [[supervised by a special master under Utah R. Civ. P. 53 (confirm)]].
- Judgment for all amounts the accounting shows are due to {{Plaintiff}}, with prejudgment interest; costs; other just relief.

## Alternative-pleading notes
- **Accounting-first rule:** historically, "a partner cannot bring an action at law concerning partnership transactions against a co-partner without first seeking an accounting." *Charlesworth*, 2005 UT App 214, ¶ 17. The rule has exceptions (¶¶ 21–23), and the defense can be lost if a defendant fails to raise it properly (¶ 23). The Utah Supreme Court declined to extend this "exclusivity rule" to LLCs. *OLP, L.L.C. v. Burningham*, 2009 UT 75, ¶ 18, 225 P.3d 177. Pleading ACC-01 together with the damages counts protects against the rule.
- When legal and equitable claims share operative facts, the jury decides the shared facts first (*OLP*, 2009 UT 75, ¶ 26). Plan the jury demand.

## Prerequisites / bars / common defenses
- **Defenses:** no fiduciary relationship; an adequate remedy at law; accounts already settled; laches.
