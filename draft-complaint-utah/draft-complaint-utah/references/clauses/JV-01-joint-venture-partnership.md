# JV-01: Breach of Joint Venture / Partnership Agreement (Utah)
Status: Verified 2026-09-21 | Last reviewed by: Claude (AI). Attorney review required.

## When to use / when not to
- **Use** when two or more parties combined money, property, skill, or labor in a common business undertaking for profit (often a single project) and the defendant failed to share profits, contribute, or account as agreed.
- A joint venture is treated much like a partnership. For an ongoing business co-owned for profit, a partnership may exist "whether or not the persons intend to form a partnership," and receiving a share of profits creates a rebuttable presumption of partnership (Utah Code § 48-1d-202(1), (3)(c)).
- **Do not use** when the relationship was really a loan, an investment with no loss-sharing, or employment; use BOC-01 instead. For an LLC, statutory claims under Title 48, Chapter 3a apply [[research]].

## Elements
Existence of the venture (from *Bassett v. Baker*, 530 P.2d 1 (Utah 1974), as restated in *Ellsworth Paulsen Constr. Co. v. 51-SPR-L.L.C.*, 2008 UT 28, ¶¶ 15–16, 183 P.3d 248):
E1. The parties combined their property, money, effects, skill, labor, and knowledge in a common undertaking for profit, with a community of interest in performing the common purpose.
E2. A joint proprietary interest in the subject matter.
E3. A mutual right to control.
E4. A right to share in the profits.
E5. Unless the parties agreed otherwise, a duty to share in any losses. Loss sharing is "a critical distinction between an investment-type relationship . . . and a joint venture." Whether there is a duty to share losses is a question of fact; it can be shown by an express provision or by evidence of the parties' conduct. *Ellsworth Paulsen*, 2008 UT 28, ¶¶ 16–18.
Breach and damages:
E6. {{Defendant}} breached the venture agreement (for example, by failing to pay {{Plaintiff}}'s share of profits or to return capital), causing damages. Contract elements apply: *America West Bank Members, L.C. v. State*, 2014 UT 49, 342 P.3d 224, 230–31.
Formality: "formality of agreement is less important than the acts and conduct of the parties." *Score v. Wilson*, 611 P.2d 367, 369 (Utah 1980).

2. {{Plaintiff}} contributed {{money, property, skill, labor}}; {{Defendant}} contributed {{contributions}}. [E1]
3. The parties jointly {{owned / held rights in}} {{subject matter}}. [E2]
4. Each party had the right to participate in decisions about {{matters}}, and did so by {{examples}}. [E3]
5. The parties agreed to share profits {{ratio}}. [E4]
6. The parties agreed to share losses {{ratio}} [[or: acted in ways showing a shared duty to bear losses, e.g., each funded cost overruns]]. [E5]
7. On or about {{Date}}, the Venture {{sold the property / received revenue}} of {{Amount}}. [E6]

**Count {{N}}: Breach of Joint Venture Agreement**
9. {{Plaintiff}} incorporates the preceding paragraphs.

11. {{Plaintiff}} performed its obligations to the Venture. [E6]
12. {{Defendant}} breached the joint venture agreement by {{breach}}. [E6]
13. {{Plaintiff}} has been damaged in the amount of at least {{Amount}}. [E6]

## Prayer language
- Judgment for {{Plaintiff}}'s share of profits and capital, at least {{Amount}}; an accounting (see ACC-01); prejudgment interest; costs; fees if an agreement provides for them.

## Alternative-pleading notes
- Pair with FD-01 (venturers and partners owe fiduciary duties; see Utah Code § 48-1d-405 for partners' duties of loyalty and care) and ACC-01.
- If the fact-finder finds no venture (often because loss sharing is missing), plead BOC-01 (as an ordinary profit-sharing contract) and UE-01 in the alternative.
- A partner "may maintain an action against the partnership or another partner for legal or equitable relief, with or without an accounting" (Utah Code § 48-1d-406(2)).

## Prerequisites / bars / common defenses
- **Statute of frauds:** a venture agreement that by its terms cannot be performed within one year must be in writing (Utah Code § 25-5-4(1)(a)). Agreements creating or transferring interests in land may implicate §§ 25-5-1 and 25-5-3. [[Research whether Utah treats an oral venture to deal in land as outside the statute; this library did not verify a case on that point. Lynch v. MacDonald, 12 Utah 2d 427, 367 P.2d 464 (1962), exists, but no statute-of-frauds discussion was found in it.]]
- **Defenses:** no loss-sharing, so the relationship was an investment or loan; no mutual control; accounting-first rule (see ACC-01).
