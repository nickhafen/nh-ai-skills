# BOC-01: Breach of Contract, Express, Written or Oral (Utah)
Status: Verified 2026-09-21 | Last reviewed by: Claude (AI). Attorney review required.

## When to use / when not to
- **Use** when the parties made an express agreement, written or oral, the plaintiff performed or was excused from performing, and the defendant failed to perform a promised term.
- **Do not use as the only count** when it is doubtful that a contract was formed (for example, missing essential terms or no mutual assent) or when the statute of frauds may apply. Add BOC-02, UE-01, or PE-01 in the alternative.
- If the agreement is a joint venture or partnership agreement, see JV-01.

## Elements
E1. A contract existed between {{Plaintiff}} and {{Defendant}}. Authority: *America West Bank Members, L.C. v. State*, 2014 UT 49, 342 P.3d 224, 230–31.
E2. The party seeking recovery performed. Authority: same.
E3. The other party breached the contract. Authority: same.
E4. Damages. Authority: same.
Pleading standard: to state the claim, a party must "allege[] sufficient facts . . . to satisfy each element." *Id.* at 230.

## Alternative-pleading notes
- Utah R. Civ. P. 8(e) allows claims stated "alternately or hypothetically" and "regardless of consistency."
- If the defense may argue that no contract was formed or that the contract is unenforceable, plead BOC-02, UE-01, and/or PE-01 in the alternative. Unjust enrichment "presupposes that no enforceable written or oral contract exists" (*Ashby v. Ashby*, 2010 UT 7, ¶ 14, 227 P.3d 246). So preface the alternative counts with "In the alternative, and only if the Court finds no enforceable contract governs ___."
- GFD-01 is a companion count that depends on this contract.

## Prerequisites / bars / common defenses
- **Statute of frauds:** Utah Code § 25-5-4(1) (for example, agreements not performable within one year, promises to answer for another's debt, real-estate commission agreements, credit agreements); §§ 25-5-1 and 25-5-3 (interests in land; leases over one year). Plead the writing or the facts that take the agreement out of the statute.
- **Contractor licensure:** a contractor may not "commence or maintain any action . . . for collection of compensation" without "alleging and proving" that it was licensed when the contract was entered into and when the cause of action arose (Utah Code § 58-55-604). Include ¶ 5.
- **Common defenses:** failure of a condition precedent, prior material breach, waiver or modification, accord and satisfaction, and failure to mitigate.
