# BOC-02: Breach of Contract Implied in Fact (Utah)
Status: Verified 2026-09-21 | Last reviewed by: Claude (AI). Attorney review required.

## When to use / when not to
- **Use** when the defendant asked for work or services, the parties never expressly agreed on all terms, and the conduct shows that both understood the work would be paid for. A contract implied in fact is "a 'contract' established by conduct" and is the second branch of quantum meruit in Utah.
- **Do not use** when an express contract clearly covers the same subject; use BOC-01. Do not use when the defendant never requested the work; consider UE-01 (implied in law) instead.

## Elements
E1. {{Defendant}} requested {{Plaintiff}} to perform work. Authority: *Davies v. Olson*, 746 P.2d 264, 269 (Utah Ct. App. 1987).
E2. {{Plaintiff}} expected {{Defendant}} to compensate {{Plaintiff}} for those services. Authority: same.
E3. {{Defendant}} knew or should have known that {{Plaintiff}} expected compensation. Authority: same.
Measure of recovery: "the amount the parties intended as the contract price"; if that amount was never stated, courts infer "the reasonable market value of the plaintiff's services." *Id.* at 269 (quoting a law review article).
Branch classification: *E & M Sales West, Inc. v. Bechtel Jacobs Co.*, 2009 UT App 299, ¶ 8 & n.4, 221 P.3d 838 (two branches of quantum meruit; implied-in-fact claims are "separate and distinct from unjust enrichment claims").

## Pleading paragraphs (template)
**General Allegations**
1. On or about {{Date}}, {{Defendant}} [[through {{agent name and title}}]] asked {{Plaintiff}} to {{describe work or services}}. [E1]
2. The request was made {{how: orally at a meeting, by email, by work order}}. [E1]
3. {{Plaintiff}} is in the business of {{business}} and performs such work for pay. {{Plaintiff}} did not offer to perform the work for free. [E2]
4. {{Plaintiff}} told {{Defendant}} [[or: {{Defendant}} knew from {{prior dealings / invoices / industry practice}}]] that {{Plaintiff}} expected to be paid for the work. [E2, E3]
5. Between {{Date}} and {{Date}}, {{Plaintiff}} performed the requested work, including {{specifics}}. {{Defendant}} accepted the work [[and used it for ___]]. [E1, E3]
6. {{Plaintiff}} invoiced {{Defendant}} {{Amount}} on {{Date}}. That amount is [[the price the parties discussed / the reasonable market value of the work]]. [Measure]
7. {{Defendant}} has not paid. [Breach and damages]

**Count {{N}}: Breach of Contract Implied in Fact [[pleaded in the alternative to Count {{BOC-01 count}}]]**
8. {{Plaintiff}} incorporates the preceding paragraphs.
9. {{Defendant}} requested that {{Plaintiff}} perform the work described above. [E1]
10. {{Plaintiff}} expected {{Defendant}} to pay for that work. [E2]
11. {{Defendant}} knew or should have known that {{Plaintiff}} expected to be paid. [E3]
12. By their conduct, the parties formed a contract implied in fact. {{Defendant}} breached it by failing to pay, and {{Plaintiff}} has been damaged in the amount of {{Amount}} [[or the reasonable value of the work, to be proven at trial]].

## Prayer language
- Judgment for {{Amount}}, or [[the agreed price / the reasonable value of the services]] as proven at trial.
- Prejudgment interest [[confirm the damages are fixed and calculable]] at the legal rate under Utah Code § 15-1-1, costs, and any other just relief.
- No attorney fees unless a statute provides for them. There is usually no fee clause in an implied contract.

## Alternative-pleading notes
- This is usually an alternative to BOC-01 (Rule 8(e)). Label it that way.
- It is distinct from UE-01. UE-01 does not require a request by the defendant, but it does require an inequitable retention of a benefit. Pleading both is common. Keep the elements separate; E & M Sales notes that courts have blurred these labels.
- Like UE-01, an implied contract generally cannot coexist with an express contract on the same subject. Say "in the alternative, if no express contract governs ___."

## Prerequisites / bars / common defenses
- **Contractor licensure:** Utah Code § 58-55-604 applies to "any action . . . for collection of compensation." Plead licensure.
- **Gratuitous services:** services performed without an expectation of payment are not recoverable. See *Dementas v. Estate of Tallas*, 764 P.2d 628 (Utah Ct. App. 1988) (finding that services were performed gratuitously defeated recovery). Plead facts showing that payment was expected.
- **Statute of frauds:** the same writing requirements apply as for BOC-01 when the subject is within Utah Code § 25-5-4 or § 25-5-3.
