# NM-01: Negligent Misrepresentation (Utah)
Status: Verified 2026-09-21 | Last reviewed by: Claude (AI). Attorney review required.

## When to use / when not to
- **Use** when {{Defendant}}, who had a financial (pecuniary) interest in the transaction and was in a better position to know the facts, carelessly misstated a material fact that {{Defendant}} should have expected {{Plaintiff}} to rely on, and {{Plaintiff}} reasonably relied on it to {{Plaintiff}}'s loss.
- It is the usual alternative to FRD-01 when intent or knowledge may be hard to prove. The elements resemble fraud, except that negligent misrepresentation "does not require the intentional mental state necessary to establish fraud." *Shah v. Intermountain Healthcare, Inc.*, 2013 UT App 261, ¶ 11, 314 P.3d 1079 (quoting *Price-Orem*).
- **Do not use** when the parties' contract governs the subject and no independent duty exists (economic loss rule), or when the defendant had no pecuniary interest.

## Elements
Authority for E1–E5: *Price-Orem Inv. Co. v. Rollins, Brown & Gunnell, Inc.*, 713 P.2d 55 (Utah 1986) (following *Jardine v. Brunswick Corp.*, 18 Utah 2d 378, 423 P.2d 659 (1967)); see also *Smith v. Frandsen*, 2004 UT 55, ¶ 9, 94 P.3d 919.
E1. {{Defendant}} carelessly or negligently made a misrepresentation of a material fact.
E2. {{Defendant}} had a pecuniary interest in the transaction.
E3. {{Defendant}} was in a superior position to know the material facts.
E4. {{Defendant}} should reasonably have foreseen that {{Plaintiff}} was likely to rely on the fact.
E5. {{Plaintiff}} reasonably relied on the misrepresentation and was injured as a result.

## Rule 9(c) particularity
Utah courts apply the particularity requirement to negligent misrepresentation as a "fraud-based" claim. *Shah*, 2013 UT App 261, ¶¶ 10–12. Complete the who/what/when/where/how checklist in FRD-01 (except the knowledge and intent items) before drafting.

## Pleading paragraphs (template)
**General Allegations**
1. On {{Date}}, at {{place / by medium}}, {{Speaker}}, {{title}} of {{Defendant}}, stated to {{Plaintiff's representative}} that {{statement}}. [E1; who, what, when, where]
2. The statement was false. In fact, {{true facts as of that date}}. [E1]
3. {{Defendant}} made the statement without reasonable care. For example, it {{failed to check ___ / ignored ___}}. [E1]
4. {{Defendant}} stood to gain from the transaction because {{commission / sale price / fee}}. [E2]
5. {{Defendant}} was in a better position than {{Plaintiff}} to know the truth because {{access to records, expertise, control of the property}}. [E3]
6. {{Defendant}} knew {{Plaintiff}} would use the statement to decide whether to {{act}}. [E4]
7. {{Plaintiff}} reasonably relied on the statement and {{act, date, amount}}. [E5]
8. As a result, {{Plaintiff}} lost {{Amount}}. [E5]

**Count {{N}}: Negligent Misrepresentation (in the alternative to Count {{FRD-01}})**
9. {{Plaintiff}} incorporates the preceding paragraphs.
10. {{Defendant}} carelessly or negligently misrepresented the material facts described in paragraphs {{1–3}}. [E1]
11. {{Defendant}} had a pecuniary interest in the transaction and was in a superior position to know the facts. [E2, E3]
12. {{Defendant}} should reasonably have foreseen that {{Plaintiff}} would rely on the misrepresentation. [E4]
13. {{Plaintiff}} reasonably relied on the misrepresentation and has been damaged in an amount to be proven at trial, at least {{Amount}}. [E5]

## Prayer language
Compensatory damages of at least {{Amount}}; prejudgment interest [[confirm]]; costs; other just relief. (Punitive damages generally do not fit a negligence theory.)

## Alternative-pleading notes
- Plead as the alternative to FRD-01 on the same General Allegations (Utah R. Civ. P. 8(e)).
- Next to BOC-01, expect an economic-loss-rule challenge. Identify an independent duty (for example, a professional or fiduciary relationship) or facts outside the contract. *HealthBanc Int'l, LLC v. Synergy Worldwide, Inc.*, 2018 UT 61, ¶ 15, 435 P.3d 193.

## Prerequisites / bars / common defenses
- **Defenses:** no pecuniary interest; opinion or prediction rather than fact; unreasonable reliance; lack of particularity; the economic loss rule; comparative fault [[research]].
