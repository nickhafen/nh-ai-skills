# FRD-01: Fraud, Intentional Misrepresentation (Utah)
Status: Verified 2026-09-21 | Last reviewed by: Claude (AI). Attorney review required.

## When to use / when not to
- **Use** when {{Defendant}} knowingly or recklessly made a false statement of a presently existing material fact to induce {{Plaintiff}} to act, and {{Plaintiff}} reasonably relied on it to {{Plaintiff}}'s injury.
- **Do not use** when the only "misrepresentation" is a broken promise that duplicates a contract term. The economic loss rule bars a fraudulent-inducement claim that "overlaps completely with a contract claim." *HealthBanc Int'l, LLC v. Synergy Worldwide, Inc.*, 2018 UT 61, ¶¶ 9–10, 435 P.3d 193. Do not plead fraud without facts that meet Rule 9(c).

## Elements
Authority for E1–E9: *Armed Forces Ins. Exch. v. Harrison*, 2003 UT 14, ¶ 16, 70 P.3d 35.
E1. A representation was made
E2. concerning a presently existing material fact
E3. that was false, and
E4. that the representor either (a) knew to be false or (b) made recklessly, knowing there was not enough knowledge to base it on,
E5. for the purpose of inducing the other party to act on it,
E6. and the other party, acting reasonably and without knowing it was false,
E7. did in fact rely on it
E8. and was induced to act
E9. to that party's injury and damage.
(Armed Forces quotes *Gold Standard, Inc. v. Getty Oil Co.*, 915 P.2d 1060, 1066–67 (Utah 1996).)

**"Presently existing fact" limit.** A promise or prediction about the future is not a statement of existing fact. However, "a promise accompanied by the present intention not to perform it, made for the purpose of deceiving the promisee," is actionable. *Cerritos Trucking Co. v. Utah Venture No. 1*, 645 P.2d 608 (Utah 1982). If the claim rests on a promise, plead specific facts showing that {{Defendant}} did not intend to perform when the promise was made.

## Rule 9(c) particularity checklist (complete before drafting)
Utah R. Civ. P. 9(c): "a party must state with particularity the circumstances constituting fraud or mistake. Malice, intent, knowledge, and other conditions of a person's mind may be alleged generally." Conclusory allegations of "fraud" are not enough; "the pleading must describe the nature or substance of the acts or words complained of." *Williams v. State Farm Ins. Co.*, 656 P.2d 966 (Utah 1982). The complaint must connect specific defendants to specific statements. *Shah v. Intermountain Healthcare, Inc.*, 2013 UT App 261, ¶¶ 10–12, 314 P.3d 1079 (citing the "who, what, when, where, and how" formulation).
- [ ] **Who:** the individual speaker (name and title) and the defendant on whose behalf the speaker acted. List each defendant separately.
- [ ] **What:** the exact words, or their substance, quoted where possible; the document if written.
- [ ] **When:** the date (or a narrow date range).
- [ ] **Where / how:** the place or medium (meeting at ___, email to ___, phone call, marketing packet).
- [ ] **Why false:** the true facts when the statement was made, and how {{Defendant}} knew or was reckless.
- [ ] **Reliance:** what {{Plaintiff}} did because of the statement, and why that reliance was reasonable.
- [ ] **Damages:** the resulting loss, stated specifically (Rule 9(h) for special damages).
- [ ] **Existing fact:** confirm the statement concerned a past or present fact, or plead present intent not to perform.

8. As a result, {{Plaintiff}} lost {{Amount}}, consisting of {{itemization}}. [E9]

**Count {{N}}: Fraud**

## Alternative-pleading notes
- Plead NM-01 in the alternative (same facts; negligent rather than knowing or reckless).
- Next to BOC-01, identify a duty or misrepresentation that is independent of the contract terms, or expect an economic-loss-rule motion (*HealthBanc*).

## Prerequisites / bars / common defenses
- **Defenses:** lack of particularity; unreasonable reliance; puffery or opinion; a future promise; an integration clause [[research]]; the economic loss rule.
