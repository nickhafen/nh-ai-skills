# UE-01: Unjust Enrichment / Quantum Meruit, Contract Implied in Law (Utah)
Status: Verified 2026-09-21 | Last reviewed by: Claude (AI). Attorney review required.

## When to use / when not to
- **Use** when {{Plaintiff}} conferred a benefit that {{Defendant}} knowingly accepted or kept, and it would be inequitable for {{Defendant}} to keep it without paying, but there is no enforceable contract (or its existence is disputed).
- **Utah's two branches of quantum meruit.** Branch 1 is the contract implied in law (unjust enrichment, quasi-contract). Branch 2 is the contract implied in fact (see BOC-02). *Davies v. Olson*, 746 P.2d 264, 268–69 (Utah Ct. App. 1987); *E & M Sales West, Inc. v. Bechtel Jacobs Co.*, 2009 UT App 299, ¶ 8, 221 P.3d 838. This file covers Branch 1. Plead Branch 2 separately as BOC-02 when the defendant requested the work.
- **Do not use** when an enforceable express contract governs the same subject (see Bars), or when the benefit was given as a gift or volunteered.

## Elements
E1. A benefit conferred on {{Defendant}} by {{Plaintiff}}. Authority: *Jeffs v. Stubbs*, 970 P.2d 1234, 1248 (Utah 1998).
E2. {{Defendant}} appreciated or knew of the benefit. Authority: same.
E3. {{Defendant}} accepted or kept the benefit under circumstances that make it inequitable to keep it without paying its value. Authority: same.
Measure: the value of the benefit to the defendant, which may differ from the plaintiff's cost or the reasonable value of the services. *Davies*, 746 P.2d at 269 [[confirm the pinpoint for the measure-of-damages discussion]].

## Prayer language
- Judgment for the value of the benefit conferred, at least {{Amount}}, as proven at trial.
- Prejudgment interest if the amount is fixed and calculable [[confirm]], costs, and other just relief. [[A constructive trust or equitable lien over specific property may be available; research before pleading.]]

## Alternative-pleading notes
- A prerequisite is "the absence of an enforceable contract governing the rights and obligations of the parties relating to the conduct at issue," and recovery "presupposes that no enforceable written or oral contract exists." *Ashby v. Ashby*, 2010 UT 7, ¶ 14, 227 P.3d 246. Plead it expressly in the alternative to BOC-01 and BOC-02 (Utah R. Civ. P. 8(e)).
- If the fact-finder finds an enforceable contract covering the subject, this count fails. Draft the facts so that both theories stand on the same General Allegations.

## Prerequisites / bars / common defenses
- **Gratuitous benefit:** benefits "officiously or gratuitously furnished are not recoverable"; a person acts gratuitously when "there was no expectation of a return benefit, compensation, or consideration." *Hess v. Johnston*, 2007 UT App 213, 163 P.3d 747 (quoting *Jeffs*). Plead the expectation of payment (¶ 4).
- **Express contract bar:** see *Ashby*, above.
- **Contractor licensure** (Utah Code § 58-55-604) applies to "any action . . . for collection of compensation," which may include quantum meruit. Plead licensure if relevant.
