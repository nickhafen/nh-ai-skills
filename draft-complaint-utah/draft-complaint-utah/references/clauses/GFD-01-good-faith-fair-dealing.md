# GFD-01: Breach of Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing (Utah)
Status: Verified 2026-09-21 | Last reviewed by: Claude (AI). Attorney review required.

## When to use / when not to
- **Use** alongside BOC-01 when the defendant used discretion under the contract, or acted in ways the contract does not expressly forbid, in order to deny {{Plaintiff}} the benefit of the bargain (for example, blocking a condition, manipulating a measure of payment, or steering business away).
- **Do not use** to add terms the parties did not agree to, or to override an express term. Utah courts reject that use (see E3). Do not plead it without a contract; the covenant exists only inside a contract.

## Elements
E1. A contract existed between the parties; the covenant is implied in it. Authority: *Oakwood Village LLC v. Albertsons, Inc.*, 2004 UT 101, ¶ 45, 104 P.3d 1226 (covenant "inheres in almost every contract").
E2. {{Defendant}} intentionally or purposely did something that destroyed or injured {{Plaintiff}}'s right to receive the fruits of the contract. Authority: *St. Benedict's Dev. Co. v. St. Benedict's Hosp.*, 811 P.2d 194, 199 (Utah 1991).
E3. The conduct is consistent with the express terms and with the parties' agreed purposes; the covenant cannot "establish new, independent rights or duties to which the parties did not agree ex ante." Authority: *Oakwood Village*, 2004 UT 101, ¶ 45; *Young Living Essential Oils, LC v. Marin*, 2011 UT 64, ¶ 12, 266 P.3d 814 (refusing to impose "our own sense of commercial morality at the expense of the express terms of the parties' contract").
E4. Damages caused by the conduct. Authority: this is a contract claim, so contract damages elements apply. [[Confirm with a specific case if challenged; this library did not verify a separate damages element.]]

## Pleading paragraphs (template)
**General Allegations** (add to BOC-01 facts)
1. The {{Agreement}} gave {{Defendant}} [[discretion over / control of]] {{subject, e.g., calculating commissions, approving plans, marketing the property}}. [E1, E3]
2. Both parties understood that the purpose of the {{Agreement}} was {{purpose}}, and that {{Plaintiff}} would receive {{the expected benefit}}. [E2, E3]
3. On or about {{Date}}, {{Defendant}} {{specific conduct}}. [E2]
4. {{Defendant}} did this to {{improper purpose, e.g., avoid paying the commission}}, and not for any legitimate business reason related to the {{Agreement}}. [E2]
5. As a result, {{Plaintiff}} lost {{benefit}}, worth at least {{Amount}}. [E4]

**Count {{N}}: Breach of the Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing**
6. {{Plaintiff}} incorporates the preceding paragraphs.
7. The {{Agreement}} contains an implied covenant that each party will not intentionally or purposely act to destroy or injure the other party's right to receive the benefits of the {{Agreement}}. [E1, E2]
8. {{Defendant}}'s conduct described in paragraphs {{3–4}} was intentional and purposeful. It is consistent with the express terms of the {{Agreement}}, yet it deprived {{Plaintiff}} of the benefits the parties agreed {{Plaintiff}} would receive. [E2, E3]
9. {{Defendant}}'s breach of the implied covenant caused {{Plaintiff}} damages of at least {{Amount}}. [E4]

## Prayer language
Same as BOC-01: contract damages, prejudgment interest, fees only if the contract or a statute provides for them, costs. Contract damages generally do not include tort remedies such as punitive damages [[confirm for the facts]].

## Alternative-pleading notes
- Plead as a separate count after BOC-01. Explain how it differs: BOC-01 addresses breach of express terms, and GFD-01 addresses conduct within the terms that defeats their purpose. If the conduct breaches an express term, BOC-01 is the better vehicle; the GFD count may be dismissed as duplicative [[confirm current Utah treatment]].
- Not available if the fact-finder finds no contract. It fails along with BOC-01, so it is not an alternative to UE-01.

## Prerequisites / bars / common defenses
- **Defenses:** the express terms authorize the conduct; the conduct was within bargained-for discretion; no damages.
- Commercial contracts may also carry a statutory good-faith duty under the Uniform Commercial Code (St. Benedict's cited the former § 70A-1-203) [[confirm the current UCC section]].
