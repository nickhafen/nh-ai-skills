# PE-01: Promissory Estoppel (Utah)
Status: Verified 2026-09-21 | Last reviewed by: Claude (AI). Attorney review required.

## When to use / when not to
- **Use** when {{Defendant}} made a clear promise about future conduct, {{Plaintiff}} reasonably relied on it to {{Plaintiff}}'s loss, and {{Defendant}} knew of the reliance, but a contract cannot be proved (for example, no consideration, indefinite terms, or a statute-of-frauds problem [[research whether PE can avoid the statute on these facts]]).
- Utah has adopted Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 90. *Skanchy v. Calcados Ortope SA*, 952 P.2d 1071, 1076–77 (Utah 1998).
- Promissory estoppel is a cause of action (a "sword"); equitable estoppel is generally a defense. *Youngblood v. Auto-Owners Ins. Co.*, 2007 UT 28, ¶¶ 15–19, 158 P.3d 1088.
- **Do not use** when an enforceable contract covers the promise; use BOC-01.

## Elements
E1. {{Plaintiff}} acted with prudence and in reasonable reliance on a promise made by {{Defendant}}. Authority: *Youngblood*, 2007 UT 28, ¶ 16, 158 P.3d at 1092.
E2. {{Defendant}} knew that {{Plaintiff}} had relied on the promise, which {{Defendant}} should reasonably have expected to induce action or forbearance by {{Plaintiff}} or a third person. Authority: same.
E3. {{Defendant}} was aware of all material facts. Authority: same.
E4. {{Plaintiff}} relied on the promise, and the reliance caused a loss to {{Plaintiff}}. Authority: same.
Note: Utah decisions also require that the promise be reasonably certain and definite [[UNVERIFIED in this library; research before relying]].

## Prayer language
- Judgment for {{Plaintiff}}'s reliance damages of at least {{Amount}}, [[or enforcement of the promise to the extent justice requires]], as proven at trial.
- Prejudgment interest if the amount is fixed [[confirm]], costs, and other just relief.

## Alternative-pleading notes
- Plead as an alternative to BOC-01 (Utah R. Civ. P. 8(e)). The Tenth Circuit description quoted in *Youngblood* ¶ 18 says the claim "arises in instances where no formal contract exists."
- Often pleaded next to UE-01. Keep the theories distinct: PE rests on a promise and reliance; UE rests on a benefit conferred.

## Prerequisites / bars / common defenses
- **Gratuitous promise:** reliance must be reasonable. See *Hess v. Johnston*, 2007 UT App 213, 163 P.3d 747 (addresses promissory estoppel after unjust enrichment; [[pinpoint not captured]]).
- **Common defenses:** the promise was indefinite; the reliance was unreasonable; an express contract controls; no detriment.
